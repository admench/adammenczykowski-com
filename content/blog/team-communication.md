## Introduction

- Team communication can have a massive positive AND negative effect on my day.
- If I communicate well, letting people know what I'm doing, people can react to that.
- If everybody else communicates well, then I can react to that too.
- Team members' **mood** is affected by communication
- Team members' **productivity** is affected by communication

> This blog post is basically a big list of tips, not longform text, hope it helps you!

Now I'm going to go into more detail on how you can work better by communicating better.

> Side note: Sometimes I'll use examples that are directly related to software development or design, however you can take your own inspiration from these points, whatever industry you are in!

## Overall points

1. Communicate regularly
   1. By keeping each other up to date on **anything** - even the small things, you stay in **sync**.
   2. If people are in sync, they are **engaged** with each other, and in a remote work environment, this is difficult to maintain! **regular** communication helps with this!
   3. Use a tool to for issues and discussions - for developers, GitHub or Microsoft's Azure are excellent tools, but there are **plenty** of options out there. Find one and _stick with it_.
   4. Optionally, your team could use a TOOL for REALTIME chat - SLACK or MICROSOFT TEAMS are good examples but there are plenty of options out there.
   5. Async communication should be your primary focus i.e. GITHUB ISSUES / GITHUB PULL REQUESTS / EMAIL. This approach forces you to think more carefully and be more descriptive.
2. THANK others & be kind
   1. This SMALL GESTURE is one of the MOST EFFECTIVE ways to improve your RAPPORT with team members
   2. USE EMPATHY - Imagine YOU were on the other side - THAT person may have WORKED HARD on something and will FEEL ENRICHED by your APPRECIATION.
   3. Also if someone is having a bad day and it is showing in their work or communication, always aim to be kind to them.
   4. Consider having a dedicated KUDOS channel in your team chat where you can celebrate other people's efforts publicly. They will feel great when they hear you praising their efforts.
3. Best practices for delegating work
   1. Delegation (the art of passing tasks onto another person) is hard if you're used to working on your own.
      1. PERHAPS You might think ANOTHER PERSON won't deliver as good a quality result as you would. However if you clearly write out the task and provide a positive feedback cycle, then it is MUCH MORE LIKELY that the end result will exceed your initial expectations.
      2. Collaboration can result in better results than solo efforts most of the time.
   2. Giving CLEAR INSTRUCTIONS is the first BEST step to achieving success when delegating. Leverage examples if possible.
   3. Don't forget to LEAVE ROOM for inspiration / creativity on the other person's part - often the fun in work is having room to breathe on a task, being able with ideas of their own.
4. Showing what you have been working on visually
   1. Celebrate the small wins with photos or screenshots of what you have been working on is a fantastic way of telling everyone what you've been up to.
   2. The act of sharing work with others naturally pushes you to want to deliver a better result. If you are truly proud of what you have done, then sharing is simply the fun part at the end of the process!
   3. Use annotation (drawing arrows and labels) in your screenshots to further clarify the change in what you have done - e.g. Skitch. I like to use BIG PINK arrows and notes to really highlight stuff.
5. Sharing a PROBLEM often attracts help
   1. If you share the problem visually (screenshots or photos) with teammates it is often reacted to with helpful comments.
   2. Ask stupid questions
   3. If you're really stuck, limit the time you spend on the issue before reaching out. Fresh eyes often find issues that you have missed. Also if you're inexperienced in the area of work, seeking help will educate and enlighten you.
6. Moaning or complaining won’t earn you any friends
   1. You DON'T KNOW what frame of mind the other person might be in today, especially when working remotely, so BE NICE
   2. Offer solutions instead of criticizing someone's work.
   3. Put your ego away
   4. If you're frustrated with the way something is or isn't happening, try to contribute to fixing the problem. Put a positive spin on things, observe how things could improve if it was done a different way, it fosters a better team atmosphere, others will then do the same! Then everybody is HAPPY! :-)
